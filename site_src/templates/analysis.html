Economic analysis

Three exploratory modules that put the nighttime-light panel next to documented economic data: the IMF World Economic Outlook classification (April 2023), World Bank WDI population and GDP, and DOSE subnational GDP. Every chart is drawn from processed files you can download; every estimate below was produced by pipeline/build_analysis.py. These are associations, not causal effects, and nighttime radiance is not a measure of GDP.

Sources and caveats

  • Lights: official annual Black Marble composites (World Bank Space2Stats 0066940) aggregated exactly from districts. National values are sums over all ADM1 units of a code; China excludes the source's “Taiwan Sheng” unit, which is reported as Taiwan to match the IMF and WDI economy definitions.
  • IMF: World Economic Outlook, April 2023, Statistical Appendix, Tables B and E (41 advanced, 155 emerging market and developing economies), parsed from the IMF PDF. Applied unchanged to every year.
  • WDI: SP.POP.TOTL, NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD, NY.GDP.MKTP.PP.KD from the World Bank API (retrieved , CC BY 4.0).
  • DOSE V2.14 (Wenz et al. 2023, CC BY 4.0): regional population and gross regional product per capita (constant 2015 US$), 2012–2019 overlap; lights aggregated to each DOSE region from its own districts.
  • Radiance is not GDP. It reflects lighting technology, settlement form, population, infrastructure and economic activity.
  • Mean radiance averages over all land pixels, so large sparsely settled countries and provinces score low even when their economies are large. Total radiance grows with population and area.
  • Gas flares add light unrelated to settlement; the “no flares” measure removes 5 km buffers around flaring sites.
  • Missing economic data are left missing; no GDP or population value is estimated. Logs are taken of positive values only, and excluded observations are counted.
  • Dependence: countries and regions are not independent draws. Standard errors are clustered and Moran's I of residuals is reported; where it is significant, uncertainty is understated.
