NASA Black Marble nighttime lights,
ready to merge with subnational economic data

The World Bank publishes Black Marble nighttime-light statistics for districts (ADM2). Most subnational economic data — regional GDP, human development, household surveys — are reported for states and provinces (ADM1). This dataset bridges the two: exact ADM1 aggregates on the source's World Bank units, a GADM 4.1 version keyed on GID_1, and the original ADM2 observations, each with explicit quality and matching indicators.

Three levels, clearly labelled

ADM2 · native source

The original Space2Stats district observations, untouched except that "no valid pixel" sums are set to missing instead of the source's 0. Includes medians and percentiles, which cannot be aggregated.

ADM1 · World Bank units exact

Districts nest exactly in their World Bank provinces, so sums and maxima are exact and means are exact coverage-weighted means. Country totals reconcile with the source to floating-point precision.

ADM1 · GADM 4.1 crosswalk

Whole districts assigned to the GADM province they overlap most. {{ "%.1f"|format(stats.gadm_share*100) }}% of the world's light is assigned; every unit carries a match-quality grade, and unassigned districts are listed, never guessed.

What you can do here

  • Explore any country at ADM1 or ADM2, monthly or annual, on a map, as time series, as annual changes, with and without gas-flare light, and with the underlying observation quality.
  • Export exactly the regions, years and indicators you need as CSV, or take full per-country and global files in CSV, Parquet and Stata format.
  • Merge with economic data using documented identifiers, crosswalk tables and Stata / R / Python examples.
  • Check every number: methods, validation tests and known limitations are published with the data.

What this is not

  • Not a new satellite product: values derive from the World Bank's Space2Stats aggregation of NASA Black Marble.
  • Not a GDP estimate. Nighttime lights correlate with economic activity; associations shown here are descriptive.
  • Not a substitute for raster-level extraction where you need medians, percentiles or custom boundaries (see alternatives).
